# M4 adversarial review: open findings

Four reviewers read the M4 changes (the board and link, the factory iPhone's test, the swing rig, and their integration into the line), and a skeptic per area tried to refute each finding. These 38 survived, 7 of them blocking. None is fixed in the commit that adds this file. Each entry gives where, what, and the fix the skeptic agreed with (or corrected).

## Blocking

1. **Station 2's hold-up step only ever sees the base draw: undersized capacitors pass** (`board/procedures.py:402`, board)

   _holdup opens the supply 2*ci after the INFO answer arrives. That answer reaches the host about 1-6 ms after a connection event (dongle floor 1 ms plus 0.5 ms exponential spread). So the 10 ms open always ends before the next event at 15 ms, and no radio event or resent packet ever falls inside it. power.holdup_c_needed sizes the part for that event and those packets: 43 uC, 206 uF. The step only checks the ~7.6 uC the IMU and idle draw take. It cannot refuse a capacitor anywhere above about 40 uF. The planted fault is 10 uF (sim.FAULTS['no_holdup']), so small that the base draw alone browns it out, so check 11 cannot show this gap.

   Fix: Measure the capacitance directly. With the board in a steady state whose current the SMU has just read, open the supply and time it to reset (or read the droop). Compute C = I*t/(V0 - v_min). Accept only if the guard-banded value, C*(1 - Smu.ACC) less its floor (as power.accept does), is at least power.holdup_c_needed. Compare with holdup_c_needed itself, not holdup_c_needed/(1-HOLD_TOL): the measurement already is the part's actual value. Alternatively, inject the worst case explicitly: an open spanning an anchor with the queue pre-loaded to window_charge's 'sent' packets. Also add a planted fault with a capacitor just under holdup_c_needed (e.g. 0.8-0.9x), so check 11 shows the step catching an undersized part, not just a missing one.

   Skeptic: Confirmed. Probe over seeds 50000+ with draw_board(rng,'no_holdup',C): the hold-up step passed 12 of 12 boards at 40, 60, 100 and 150 uF. It starts refusing only at about 30-35 uF. The derived requirement is 206 uF (window_charge 43.3 uC over 0.21 V). Tracing the open: no connection event fell inside it in any run, and it started 1.5-3.1 ms after the last event. The open starts 2*ci after the INFO answer arrives (event + air + 1 ms dongle floor + exp(0.5 ms)), so the 10 ms open ends before the next event 15 ms later. The charge drawn during the open was 5.4-7.9 uC, i.e. base draw only. Check 11 cannot see this because the planted 10 uF part fails on base draw alone. One correction to the proposed fix: opening across an anchor with the bat streaming still would not reach the derivation's worst case. In 10 ms the stream makes only about one packet, while window_charge assumes 3 sends, 2 of them resent losses. That would refuse only parts below about 110 uF.

2. **Planted 'tilted' fault is caught only by the luck of one seed: station 2 misses it about half the time** (`../scripts/batline/check_link.py:535`, board)

   The bat lies in a V cradle with its roll free (Lying.roll uniform over 0-360 deg). The 'gravity along' test reads only the component along the bat's axis. A board seated 10 deg off about bat-y adds -sin(10 deg)*cos(roll) to that reading. When the roll puts gravity near the tilt axis, |cos(roll)| < 0.55 and the reading stays inside the 0.095 g limit, so the mis-seated board passes every step. Check 11 runs each fault once, on seed 100+i. Seed 111 has roll 158 deg, which happens to expose the tilt, so the check passes while station 2 cannot reliably see a mis-seated board. The same single-seed design makes the step names fragile: accel_range is refused at 'gravity along' instead of 'gravity size' on another seed.

   Fix: Make the seat pitch observable at any roll: key the cradle's roll, or take the still reading at a second roll about 90 deg away and test the tilt from both readings. In check 11, run each planted fault over many seeds and poses. Require the miss rate to be no worse than the step's designed power at the binomial's Z tail (binomial_least), not one seed per fault.

   Skeptic: Confirmed. Board.IMU_AXES is the identity, the cradle's roll is uniform over 0-360 deg, and 'gravity along' reads only a@along_axis + sin(pitch). A tilt about bat-y shows up as about sin(10 deg)*cos(roll), which is zero at roll 90/270 for any tilt size. A healthy board can match the other two components at some roll, so with an unknown roll the along component is the only thing that discriminates. Probe of run_station's draw at seeds 111+1000*j, j=0..19: 9 of 20 tilted boards passed station 2 outright (ok True, last step 'sleep current'). For example seed 3111 (roll 298, along -0.092), 8111 (roll 77, -0.062) and 19111 (roll 82, -0.0035), all against a limit of 0.0951 g. Check 11's own seed, 111 (roll 158), reads 0.124 and is refused, so the check passes on the luck of one seed.

3. **Hold-up derivation applies the radio's 3 V DC/DC currents at 1.92-1.71 V** (`board/power.py:177`, board)

   spec.Mcu says its currents are typical 'at 3 V on the DC/DC'. The board runs straight from the cells (no regulator ahead of it), and window_charge, holdup_s and holdup_c_needed spend those currents and charges (Q_EVENT, Q_NOTIFY, I_TX, I_CPU, I_IDLE) unchanged while the capacitor falls from v_hold = 1.92 V to v_min = 1.71 V. A buck converter draws roughly constant input power, so input current scales with about 3 V / V_in, which is 1.56-1.75x at these voltages. On the internal LDO the currents are larger still. The firmware sim draws the same constants, so check 6 agrees with the derivation and cannot catch it. A smaller effect pushes the same way: v_hold is the cells' open-circuit voltage, but the capacitor starts at N*(V_oc - I*R_cell), and power.py itself models R_cell at 0.705 ohm a cell at 90 %. That costs 2-15 mV of the 210 mV budget.

   Fix: Give the Mcu currents their reference voltage (V_REF = 3.0). In window_charge/holdup_s, treat the MCU's draw as constant power, I*V_REF/V, and integrate from v0 down to v_min. Do the same in firmware._drain (base current) and pulse (charge scaled by V_REF/v), so check 6 exercises it. Keep the IMU's I_RUN constant. Optionally start v0 at N*(V_oc - I_mean*R_cell). Then re-derive HOLD_C: by these numbers the next E6 value whose low tolerance clears the need, 470 uF.

   Skeptic: Confirmed. spec.Mcu states its currents are typical 'at 3 V on the DC/DC', and Q_EVENT/Q_NOTIFY come from a development kit, also at 3 V. power.window_charge/holdup_s/holdup_c_needed and firmware._drain/pulse spend them unchanged between v_hold = 1.92 V and v_min = 1.71 V. Decomposition of the 10 ms window: IMU 7.00 uC, MCU 36.32 uC. With only the MCU part scaled by 3/V: x1.56 gives 63.8 uC, so 304 uF is needed; x1.75 gives 70.7 uC, so 337 uF. The low-tolerance part is 264 uF. Integrating C dV/dt = -(I_imu + P_mcu/V) with P_mcu taken at 3 V: 264 uF rides 8.27 ms and 330 uF rides 10.34 ms, against Rules.BOUNCE_S = 10 ms. The firmware sim uses the same constants, so check 6 agrees with the derivation and cannot catch it. The LDO alternative is worse still. The cell-resistance point is real but small: 0.705 ohm a cell at HOLD_LIFE costs about 2 mV at the 1.65 mA mean.

4. **Check 9's must-fail is an algebraic identity and does not run the check's test** (`../scripts/batline/check_swing.py:363`, swing)

   Check 9 passes when `pull < SR.spring_force(d, SR.SHORTEST)`. Its must-fail never calls spring_force and never uses the shortest pair. It multiplies weak_rate(d) by the dummy pack's spring length, and that product is 0.5*n*m*F_MAX by the definition of weak_rate. pull = (n*m + tip)*F_MAX is always at least twice that, so `not pull < fw` is true for every spring, geometry, F_MAX and spring_force implementation. The must-fail cannot fail, which breaks the suite's own rule that the same test must reject a wrong variant.

   Fix: Run the passing comparison on the wrong spring: `with patched(CapSpring, RATE=weak_rate(d, SR.SHORTEST)): fw = SR.spring_force(d, SR.SHORTEST)`, then `check(..., not pull < fw)`. The value still comes out as 0.5*n*m*F_MAX by construction (probe: 0.195 N), but the must-fail now goes through the same spring_force, the same pack and the same inequality as the passing check. A spring_force that ignored RATE or the pack's seated length would then make it fail.

   Skeptic: Confirmed. weak_rate(d) defaults to the DUMMY pack, so fw = weak_rate(d)*(L_FREE - seated(d, DUMMY)[1]) is exactly 0.5*n*m*1e-3*F_MAX. Probe: fw = 0.195348 N, and 0.5*n*m*F_MAX*1e-3 = 0.195348 N. pull = (n*m + MASS/3)*1e-3*F_MAX = 0.3921 N, a ratio of 2.007. Because SHORTEST.m == DUMMY.m == Cell.MASS, `not pull < fw` holds for any F_MAX > 0 and any spring constants. The must-fail never calls spring_force and never uses the SHORTEST geometry the passing test uses, so it cannot fail and tells us nothing about check 9's test. That breaks the suite's own must-fail rule (header lines 15-17).

5. **Check 5: tolerances with no stated origin, a must-fail that misses the insensitivity half, and stiffness misdescribed** (`../scripts/batline/check_swing.py:303`, swing)

   (a) `rest` uses 1e-3*fs and `moved < 1e-2`. Neither number fits the origins the suite header allows (float rounding, a differencing step, a drive cycle, a Monte Carlo standard error), and neither says where it comes from. (b) The must-fail only shows that settle() raises ValueError with RING/2. Nothing makes the 'a softer contact changes nothing' comparison (`moved < 1e-2`) fail. If settle failed in the passing branch, `s.settle(MO.park)` would crash the suite with a traceback instead of reporting a FAIL. (c) The docstring says doubling sim.RING halves the contact stiffness. solref timeconst = RING*dt/zeta, and MuJoCo's k = d/(dmax^2*timeconst^2*dampratio^2), so doubling RING quarters the stiffness and halves the natural frequency. The 'twice as stiff' must-fail is really 4x as stiff.

   Fix: Derive the insensitivity tolerance from what it protects: for example, require the change in the run's least + plate force to stay under that force's margin above zero (check 8's criterion), stated as such. Give that half its own must-fail: a contact model that does move the least force, run through the same `moved` comparison. Wrap the passing branch's settle/run in try/except ValueError and report FAIL. Reword to 'quartering the contacts' stiffness (sim.RING doubled)' and 'the contacts four times as stiff (RING halved)', and fix the detail string.

   Skeptic: (a) `rest` uses `<= 1e-3*fs` and the insensitivity half uses `moved < 1e-2`. Neither states an origin, and neither fits the origins the suite header says every tolerance has (float rounding, a differencing step, a drive cycle, an MC standard error). The 1e-3 only repeats settle()'s own hand-set stop criterion. (b) Confirmed in the code: the must-fail only checks that settle raises with RING/2. Nothing shows `moved < 1e-2` rejecting anything. settle at line 300 and run_of are not wrapped, so a settle failure in the passing branch gives a traceback, not a FAIL. That still exits non-zero, so this part is robustness only. Probe: with RING=2, settle raises and ends with contact forces [0.0, 1.239] N, as reported. (c) solref = (RING*dt/zeta, zeta), and MuJoCo's k = d/(dmax^2*timeconst^2*dampratio^2) = d/(dmax^2*RING^2*dt^2). Doubling RING therefore quarters the stiffness, and RING/2 makes it four times as stiff, not twice. The header (lines 39-43) and the detail string ('twice the softness') are wrong. (c) on its own is a minor docstring fix; (a) and (b) break the stated tolerance-origin and must-fail rules.

6. **The swing rig is timed at the gimbal's clamp and has no floor space or gantry reach** (`line.py:503`, integration)

   Station 4's new 'swing' job loads the bat into the swing rig using `load`, which is the transfer from the slots to the gimbal's own fixture centre F, with the gimbal's rise. The station's footprint and `around` come only from the gimbal rig (`rig.footprint()`, `rig.reach()`). Nothing in line.py or swing/rig.py gives the swing rig a footprint, an enclosure or a clamp pose. As a result the floor plan, the station's reach (h.points) and the module travel derived from it (station_spec.from_line) all leave out a machine that sweeps a disc about 2.2 m across, which the README places 'beside the cell'. The comment 'its clamp where the head reaches as it reaches the gimbal's' sets the station pose by hand, and that pose is physically impossible because the gimbal already occupies that point.

   Fix: In swing/rig.py, derive the rig's envelope: the disc swept about Rig.hub, radius rig.L plus the hands-to-tip length plus the enclosure clearance, and the hub height that disc needs above the floor. Also derive the clamp's pose at Motion.park (hub + L(cos phi0, sin phi0), bat level). In line._layout, place it beside the gimbal, either widening station 4's footprint or adding it as its own floor item. Time the swing job's load and unload as h.transfer(slot, swing_clamp_uv, swing_rise) so that point enters h.points and st.reach. Give from_line a region for the rig's clamp so the module's travel covers it.

   Skeptic: Confirmed in line.py:476-509. Station 4's footprint and `around` come only from rig_spec.design(d) (the gimbal and its cameras). The new swing job times its load and unload with `load = _mean_transfer(h, slots, F, rise)`, the gimbal clamp's transfer and rise. station_spec.from_line (station/spec.py:752-791) builds regions only from the docks and the gimbal's `st.work`, so no module region, travel, footprint or floor_plan item contains a swing rig. A probe on KID gives L = 0.465 m, hub [0.002, 0.470] m and 629 mm from the hands to the tip, so the arm and bat sweep a disc of about 1.09 m radius. Nothing in swing/rig.py gives a hub height, a clamp pose or an enclosure. The comment 'its clamp where the head reaches as it reaches the gimbal's' stands in for a station pose that CLAUDE.md says must be computed. floor_plan therefore also leaves out a machine about 2.2 m across from the aisle length and the robot's trips. One caveat: the claim that the pose is 'physically impossible' reads too much into the comment, which more likely means 'at the same transfer cost'. The defect is that the pose is never computed. Also note that M2 already left the rig off the floor (an est op). M4 newly borrows the gimbal's pose for its timing.

## Minor

1. **Clock offset is held to Quality.SYNC_S only at the window's mean counter, the most favourable point** (`../scripts/batline/check_link.py:754`, board)

   recovered() measures the offset error only at cm.k0, the mean counter, where the envelope's error is smallest. sync.envelope_rms, and so packets_needed and listen_s, measure the error at the same point (err = t0 - k0/spp*dt). The ClockMap is used across the whole window, though. phone/app.fuse reads cm.at(k[0]) and cm.at(k[s]) at the stance, which starts at the window's first sample. The study applies sync_ms at the contact sample k_c. Away from k0 the rate error adds to the offset, and the error passes SYNC_S. Check 17 cannot see this, and the stream length is sized on it.

   Fix: Hold the error where the map is read. Have envelope_rms (and check 17) report the error at the span's ends, or the worst over the span the consumer uses, and size packets_needed/listen_s on that. Alternatively, have the app or game recover the clock from a window centred on the samples it reads (stance end or contact), and say in sync's docstring that SYNC_S holds at the window's mean counter.

   Skeptic: The facts check out. recovered() takes the error at cm.k0, and envelope_rms takes it at k0 too (err = t0 - k0/spp*dt). A Monte Carlo of sync's own model at packets_needed (phone n=176, 3000 trials) gives an RMS of 0.955 ms at k0, 1.231 ms at the first sample and 1.244 ms at the last. Dongle n=23: 1.008 / 1.718 / 1.301 ms. SYNC_S is 1.0 ms. app.fuse reads cm.at(k[0]) and cm.at(k[s]) with spare = Z*SYNC_S, so the spare assumes SYNC_S where the error is about 25 % larger. Downgraded to minor: in this milestone the map only selects stance frames. With Z=4 the spare is still about 3.2 sigma, k[s] already sits before motion (mv[0] - mem), and the fused sweet spot does not use the clock at contact. The gap matters for the game, where the contact is read near the window's end, and for sync's docstring, which says the recovered clock is held to SYNC_S.

2. **packets_needed bisects on Monte Carlo noise; the dongle's n=23 misses the sync budget** (`board/sync.py:185`, board)

   envelope_rms runs 200 trials with a different seed for each n (12345 + n), so its value moves by several percent and is not monotone in n. packets_needed doubles, then bisects for the first n whose noisy estimate falls under the budget, which favours an underestimate. For the dongle it returns 23, but a precise estimate at n=23 is over the budget. The phone's 176 is fine (0.955 ms), but only just.

   Fix: Use common random numbers: one seed, independent of n, drawing the same per-trial stream prefix. Run enough trials that the estimate's standard error is a small share of the budget. Compare rms + Z*se (the rms's standard error from the trials) with the budget, so the noise cannot favour an underestimate.

   Skeptic: Confirmed. envelope_rms seeds with 12345+n at 200 trials. Dongle values: 22:1.042 23:0.949 24:0.966 25:1.041 26:0.888 ms, which is not monotone in n. packets_needed returns 23 for the dongle, but at 5000 trials n=23 gives 1.011 ms against a budget of sqrt(SYNC_S^2 - HOST_FLOOR_SD^2) = 0.980 ms. The true first n under budget is about 24-25. The phone's 176 measures 0.950 ms at 3000 trials, so it passes but with little margin. At 200 trials n=180 reads 0.996 and n=170 reads 0.971, so the bisection's answer is noise-driven.

3. **Check 14's 'power lost mid-commit' cuts power before the commit reaches the bat** (`../scripts/batline/check_link.py:663`, board)

   brown() opens the circuit 1 us after the REC_COMMIT request is issued, with hold_c = 1 uF. The next connection event's Q_EVENT pulse browns the board out before the central's slot delivers the commit, so the firmware never receives that commit. What the scenario really tests is losing the staged buffer, not a cut between the page erase and the write. That case is covered only at firmware level, in check 7, without BleLink's retry.

   Fix: Cut from the firmware's receipt of the commit: wrap fw._commit to call open_circuit(t + Mcu.ERASE_PAGE_S/2, t + 0.15) after the original runs. Use a hold_c that survives the event pulses until then, or schedule the open inside the erase so the next pulse lands in it. Assert that a commit ran before the brown-out with its target page erased, and that the retry then restored the record.

   Skeptic: Confirmed by code and probe. brown() opens the circuit 1 us after the REC_COMMIT request is queued, with hold_c = 1 uF. At the next event, the central's fw.pulse(Q_EVENT) (5.5 uC on 1 uF) browns the board out before the slot delivers the commit. Probe: cut at 3.0549 s, browned at 3.0609 s, and the only _commit ran at 3.7527 s (boots 3, after 1 brown-out), with 1 retry. So the scenario tests a staged buffer lost to a brown-out plus BleLink's retry. It does not test a cut between erase and write, which the code comment ('browns it out inside the commit') and the check's name claim.

4. **Packet make-time (Firmware.CPU_PACKET_S) is missing from both the sim and sync's delay floor** (`board/firmware.py:296`, board)

   The firmware charges q_cpu but queues the packet at the instant of its last sample. spec.Firmware.CPU_PACKET_S says the CPU spends 0.3 ms reading the FIFO and handing the packet to the stack. sync.floor = HOST_FLOOR_S + link_floor leaves that time out too: the host floor's lab method times from the bat sending, not from the sample. On real hardware the recovered sample times would be at least 0.3 ms late, 30 % of SYNC_S. Check 17 cannot see the bias because the sim omits the same delay.

   Fix: Queue the packet at t + FW.CPU_PACKET_S in firmware.py. Add the bat's make time to sync.floor, or define HOST_FLOOR_S's lab measurement as starting from the sample, and keep the two consistent.

   Skeptic: Confirmed. firmware._on_event charges pulse(P.q_cpu()) but queues the packet at t, the time of its last sample: self.txq.append((t, "stream", pk, None)). spec.Firmware.CPU_PACKET_S (0.3 ms) is the time spent reading the FIFO and handing the packet to the stack. sync.floor = HOST_FLOOR_S + link_floor counts from the event's opening (link air) and from air to callback (host), so the bat-side make time is in neither. On hardware the lower envelope would sit at least 0.3 ms late, and check 17 cannot see that because the sim omits the same delay.

5. **Flash retry re-programs without erasing, breaking SwdHAL.program's 'erased' precondition** (`board/procedures.py:281`, board)

   _flash calls erase_all once, then program(0, img) up to twice. The second program writes over programmed flash. Flash programming can only clear bits, and SwdHAL.program's contract says '(word-aligned, erased)'. SimSwd.program's image path simply overwrites (fw.image = data), while its UICR path correctly ANDs. So the sim accepts a retry that real flash would not, for a bit wrongly programmed to 0.

   Fix: Erase before the retry (erase_all, or the image's pages), and re-write the serial afterwards if the retry needs erase_all. Make SimSwd.program AND the image into what is there (erased = 0xFF, starting from fw.image or all-0xFF), so a retry without an erase shows its effect.

   Skeptic: Confirmed. _flash calls st.swd.erase_all() once, then program(0, img) up to twice. SwdHAL.program's contract is '(word-aligned, erased)'. Real flash can only clear bits, so a retry over programmed words cannot repair a bit wrongly left at 0. SimSwd.program overwrites the image (fw.image = data) but ANDs in the UICR path, so the sim accepts a retry real flash would not. Check 11 is unaffected because image_flip corrupts every write.

   Skeptic: Confirmed. The check is `episodes <= most`, a burst count over window_charge(w) against binomial_most. 8. **Link.LL_OVERHEAD misstates the L2CAP header and leaves out ATT's** (`spec.py:259`, board)

   The comment counts 'the L2CAP header's 2'. The L2CAP basic header is 4 bytes (length and channel ID), and a notification also carries ATT's 3-byte opcode and handle, which notification_bytes() (MTU - 3) excludes. Around a 244-byte value the true on-air overhead is 22 bytes, not 17: 244 + 3 + 4 = 251 = LL_PAYLOAD. The empty PDU used in link_floor (air_s(0)) has no L2CAP header and no MIC. Air time and q_air are a few percent off, and link_floor is off by tens of us.

   Fix: Split the constant into the link layer's own bytes (preamble 2 + access address 4 + header 2 + CRC 3, plus MIC 4 on encrypted non-empty PDUs) and the per-notification L2CAP 4 + ATT 3. Use the first alone for empty PDUs and both for notifications and control writes, in power.air_s, sync.air_s and radio's slots. Correct the comment.

   Skeptic: The comment is wrong. The L2CAP basic header is 4 bytes, not 2, and the ATT notification's 3-byte opcode and handle are not counted (MTU 247 + L2CAP 4 = 251 = LL_PAYLOAD).
