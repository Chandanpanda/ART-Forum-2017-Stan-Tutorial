WHERE THE RIG DIVERGES FROM WHAT check_drive MODELLED
  1. The rail is FOUR ARCS, not one, because a pinion's hub reaches
     into the flange's band.  A pinion fills its own window, so the
     only true dead arc is still the mouth's 59.62 deg, against the
     120 deg capture_wander was bounded at.
  2. A printed ring weighs about 2.7 g against Ring.MASS = 22 g.  It
     matters less than it looks: check_drive measures the inertial
     share of the demand at 1e-4 N.m against the thread's 0.0220, so a
     lighter ring makes the torque figure conservative, not wrong.
  3. The standoff circle (r 26.52) and the pinion circle (r 26.40) are
     0.125 mm apart, because a 6 mm bonding pad has to sit wholly on
     the skirt.  The two holes are 2.95 PRESS and 3.40 CLEARANCE and
     they look identical, so plate.dxf LABELS them.  Read the labels.
  4. The plate is LOCATED by its two tabs and is not yet FIXED by
     them: add two corner brackets or a fillet of adhesive, or it
     pivots about the line of the tabs.

BUILD NOTES
  1. Print the ring and the pinions in RESIN.  Print the rail arcs in
     FOUR clearance variants by scaling the channel: 0.05 is the spec's
     fit, and check_drive claims the design turns from 0.05 to 0.20.
     Which of the four turns IS the measurement; print all four.
  2. Bond the standoffs to the rail's skirt with the ring ASSEMBLED and
     held concentric.  Four separately-mounted arcs have no common
     register but the ring itself, so use the ring as the register.
  3. Bond the V-blocks to the base against the scribe lines, with the
     ring assembled: the scribe says where, the ring says whether.
  4. The pinion phase is SET, not printed: mesh each pinion against the
     ring, then tension the belt, then bond the motor pulley.  One
     pinion a quarter of a pitch out jams the drive (check_drive).
  5. Mark the ring, film the run, count turns against commanded steps.
     A lost tooth is the fault this rig exists to find.
